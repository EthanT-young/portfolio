Kibitz

A free chess game review that runs in your browser. Stockfish analyzes every position, each move gets a verdict, and a coach explains what the move did.

Nov 2026React · TypeScript · Stockfish (WebAssembly) · Docker
  • Open Kibitz→
  • Source on GitHub↗ (opens in a new tab)

Overview

Import a game from Chess.com or Lichess by username, or paste a PGN or a move list. Kibitz then gives you:

  • A summary report with accuracy for both players, move-quality counts, an estimated game rating, grades for the opening, middlegame and endgame, and an evaluation graph.
  • A move-by-move review with an eval bar, best-move and threat arrows, and a verdict on each move, from Brilliant down to Blunder.
  • A coach that says why a move was good or bad, for example "This allows Qg5, attacking the knight on e5 and the pawn on g2."
  • Retry and explore modes: replay your mistakes and have the coach check the new move, or drag pieces to try your own lines with live analysis.

How it works

  • The engine is Stockfish 19 compiled to WebAssembly. Several copies run in parallel Web Workers on the visitor's computer, so my server only sends static files.
  • Move verdicts come from how much a move lowered the player's winning chances. Accuracy is fitted against 96 games Chess.com had reviewed and lands within about 6 points of their number.
  • The coach is rule-based, not an AI model. It replays the engine's lines and runs small detectors for hanging pieces, forks, pins, skewers and mate threats, then describes the most important one.

On the server it is a small container: a Caddy file server with a read-only filesystem and no Linux capabilities, running as an unprivileged user behind the site's main Caddy.
